# Quick Facts Sheet: SIH26191

- **Project Name:** SIH26191 — Intelligent Identification of Hazard-Based Red Zones, Carrying Capacity Assessment, and Immediate Relocation Needs for Vulnerable Habitations
- **Problem Statement ID:** 26191 (Ministry of Home Affairs / NDRF Disaster Management Division)
- **Pilot Geographic District:** Rudraprayag District, Uttarakhand, India
- **One-Line Problem:** Mountain disaster management in high-hazard Himalayan terrain is predominantly reactive, lacking proactive spatial screening to identify exposed habitations and assess safer relocation land capacity before disasters strike.
- **One-Line Solution:** An enterprise-grade, glass-box GIS decision-support platform that deterministically screens 30m DEM terrain physics, Census demographics, and disaster history to rank 653 habitations into 4 planning horizons and extract 2,998 candidate relocation sites with PMAY-G capacity scenarios.
- **Target Users:** State Disaster Management Authorities (USDMA/SDMA), District Disaster Management Authorities (DDMA/DM), National Disaster Response Force (NDRF), MoHA, Tehsil Planners, and Rural Housing Agencies (PMAY-G).
- **Main Technologies:** Python 3.10+, FastAPI, Uvicorn, GeoPandas, Rasterio, Shapely, NumPy, SciPy, NetworkX, React 18, Vite, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Leaflet GIS, Docker, Nginx.
- **Main Models / Engines:** Deterministic 2D finite-difference slope/aspect operator, D8 flow accumulation & Topographic Wetness Index (Beven-Kirkby 1979), morphological 8-connectivity image segmentation, deterministic rule-based priority engine, and PMAY-G normative capacity scenario model.
- **Primary Datasets:** Copernicus GLO-30 DEM (30m), Census of India 2011 Primary Census Abstract (PCA), Development Data Lab SHRUG v2.2 centroids, ESA WorldCover 10m Land Cover (2021 v200), OpenStreetMap Critical Infrastructure (291 facilities), OpenStreetMap Road Network (6,397 km), and Literature-Curated Disaster Registry (22 canonical events 1998–2024).
- **Dataset Sizes & Records:**
  - Copernicus DEM: 19.05 MB (30m grid, >3.5M active pixels)
  - Census 2011 PCA: 653 rural revenue habitations (232,360 population, 50,882 households)
  - Candidate Red Zones: 289 vectorized polygons ($\ge 5,000\text{ m}^2$)
  - Candidate Relocation Areas: 2,998 polygons (8,095.52 ha, slope $\le 20^\circ$, 1–10 ha MMU)
  - Critical Infrastructure: 291 POIs (187 healthcare, 72 education, 28 civic, 4 emergency)
  - Road Network: 3,914 segments (6,397.35 km total; 4,756.84 km vehicular)
  - Historical Disasters: 22 canonical verified events (6,913 fatalities recorded)
- **Key Metrics & Cutoffs:**
  - Metric Coordinate Reference System: WGS 84 / UTM Zone 44N (`EPSG:32644`)
  - Storage Coordinate System: WGS 84 (`EPSG:4326`)
  - Multi-Hazard Combination: $M(x,y) = 0.5 \cdot \text{Terrain} + 0.5 \cdot \text{Flood}$
  - Red Zone Pixel Threshold: Multi-Hazard Class 3 ($M \ge 0.65$) with $\text{area} \ge 5,000\text{ m}^2$
  - Tier 1 Rule: $\text{direct\_zone\_overlap} == \text{True}$ OR ($d \le 500\text{m} \land \text{MH\_Class} \ge 2$)
  - Tier 2 Rule: $d \le 2,000\text{m}$ (and not Tier 1)
  - Tier 3 Rule: $d \le 5,000\text{m}$ (and not Tier 1 or 2)
  - Beyond Proximity Rule: $d > 5,000\text{m}$
  - Demographic $P_{75}$ Benchmarks: Child pop $> 15.1\%$, SC pop $> 24.6\%$, Non-worker $> 57.9\%$, Illiteracy $> 34.0\%$
  - PMAY-G Housing Standard: $25.0\text{ m}^2/\text{HH}$ built area, $40\%$ net buildable site efficiency ($\eta = 0.40$), $4.0\text{ persons/HH}$, $100\text{ ha}$ scale protection cap.
- **Key Results:**
  - **Tier 1 (Immediate Field Assessment):** 12 villages (4,750 pop, 977 HH)
  - **Tier 2 (Short-Term Planning Review):** 69 villages (23,012 pop, 4,674 HH)
  - **Tier 3 (Medium-Term Monitoring):** 204 villages (64,463 pop, 13,978 HH)
  - **Beyond Proximity (Routine Monitoring):** 368 villages (140,135 pop, 31,253 HH)
  - **Total At-Risk Population (Tiers 1 & 2):** 27,762 persons (5,651 households) across 81 villages.
  - **Top Attention Village:** Marora (Village ID 42573, distance to red zone: $42.5\text{ m}$).
  - **Candidate Feasible Relocation Area:** 2,998 polygons totaling 8,095.52 hectares.
- **Architecture:** Decoupled 3-tier architecture: Ingestion/GIS Processing Pipeline $\rightarrow$ FastAPI ASGI Microservice (with in-memory GeoPandas cache & R-tree spatial indexing) $\rightarrow$ React 18 + Vite + Leaflet GIS Command Center.
- **Main Innovation:** End-to-end coupling of multi-hazard screening with topographically feasible alternative site extraction and PMAY-G dwelling capacity scenario modeling under 100% glass-box explainable rules and live dynamic recomputation.
- **Main Advantages:**
  - Narrows district-wide field inspections from 653 habitations down to 12 high-priority targets.
  - Sub-millisecond API response latency ($<15\text{ ms}$).
  - 100% deterministic and legally auditable (no black-box AI hallucinations).
  - Open-source, zero commercial API license costs ($< \$10/\text{month}$ cloud hosting).
- **Main Limitations:**
  - 30-meter DEM resolution cannot resolve micro-topographic roadside cuts $<30\text{m}$.
  - Census demographic baseline is 2011 vintage (~15 years old).
  - Village geometry represented as administrative point centroids rather than building polygon footprints.
  - Candidate relocation sites are topographically feasible only; geotechnical soil testing and legal cadastre title verification are mandatory.
- **Deployment Status:**
  - Live Frontend: `https://sih-26191.vercel.app/` (Vercel CDN).
  - Backend API: Containerized FastAPI ASGI on Render / Railway (`/docs`).
  - Containerization: Multi-stage Docker & Docker Compose.
  - Automated Tests: 18 passed in Pytest suite (100% coverage).
- **Future Scope:** Ingestion of 1–5m drone/LiDAR DEMs, official Revenue Department cadastral ownership maps, real-time IMD gridded rainfall feeds, and a mobile field surveyor app.
